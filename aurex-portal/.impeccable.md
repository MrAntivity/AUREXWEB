## Design Context

### Users
Three distinct user groups, all in B2B/institutional contexts:

- **Researchers / lab staff (requesters)**: Scientists and lab technicians who need to order supplies efficiently. They're not procurement specialists — they want fast, frictionless browsing and ordering. Often working in labs with limited screen time, need to scan catalogs quickly.
- **Department heads / approvers**: Administrators managing budgets and approvals. Need clear visibility into spend, pending requests, and audit trails. Often reviewing orders from a desktop in an office context.
- **Finance viewers**: Monitoring-only users focused on budget data and compliance. Need dense, scannable data with clear status indicators.
- **Aurex staff (fulfillment)**: Internal team managing order fulfillment and operations. Efficiency-first — they live in the staff portal all day, need fast workflows.

The job to be done across all roles: **make institutional procurement less painful**. The emotional goal is competence — users should feel in control, not confused.

### Brand Personality
**Bold, disruptive, professional, clinical.**

Three words: **confrontational · precise · systematic**

- Confrontational: Aurex is a challenger brand taking on established players (Thermo Fisher). The brand should feel confident, not deferential. It makes choices and stands behind them.
- Precise: This is medical/research equipment. The interface must convey accuracy and reliability at every touch. No vagueness, no decorative noise.
- Systematic: Procurement is a workflow, not a shopping cart. Everything should feel like it was designed to scale — consistent, organized, purposeful.

Emotional goals:
- Marketing site: Confidence + credibility. Make an institution feel like switching to Aurex is the obvious, intelligent choice.
- Institution portal: Competence + control. Make researchers feel empowered, not lost in a procurement maze.
- Staff portal: Efficiency + clarity. Make fulfillment fast and error-free.

### Aesthetic Direction
**Theme:**
- Marketing site: Dark (established — `#0c0c13` base). Lean into it. Premium, focused, editorial.
- Institution portal: Light (established). Clinical clarity — white space is intentional, not empty.
- Staff portal: Dark. Internal tool used by staff in fulfillment contexts; benefits from a focused dark UI.

**Color approach:**
- Primary accent: Orange (#f97316) is established. Warm and energetic — right for a disruptive challenger brand. Keep it, but develop a more sophisticated system using OKLCH. Tint neutrals toward the orange hue very subtly (chroma 0.005–0.01).
- Accent should feel rare — 60% neutral surfaces, 30% secondary elements, 10% orange.
- No AI color palette: no cyan, no purple-to-blue gradients, no glowing neons.

**Typography approach:**
- Current Inter usage must change — it's the single most recognizable "we didn't decide on a font" choice.
- Brand words (confrontational · precise · systematic) point toward a condensed or engineered sans-serif for display use — something with visible structure, not the rounded friendliness of Inter.
- Body: a refined, highly legible text face with good small-size rendering for data-dense portal UIs.
- Avoid all fonts in the reflex_fonts_to_reject list. Look beyond Google Fonts defaults — check Pangram Pangram, ABC Dinamo, or Google Fonts' more obscure geometric/grotesque options.

**Reference feel:**
- NOT: generic SaaS (rounded cards, soft shadows, pastel accents, sidebar with icons and labels)
- NOT: typical medical software (gray everything, tiny fonts, overwhelming density)
- NOT: flashy startup landing page (hero animations, gradients, lots of color)
- YES: Authoritative, clinical confidence. Think precision instruments. Think a well-edited scientific journal that also happens to be beautifully designed.

### Design Principles

1. **Precision over polish** — Every element earns its place. No decorative noise. If removing a detail doesn't hurt comprehension, remove it.

2. **Challenger confidence** — Aurex competes against incumbents. The design should feel like it made deliberate, opinionated choices — not like it played it safe.

3. **System over spectacle** — Consistent, scalable patterns first. The marketing site can be more expressive; the portal should feel like a well-engineered system. Animation is purposeful, not performative.

4. **Clinical hierarchy** — Information density done right: users can scan and find what they need in seconds. Status, roles, and approvals must always be visually unambiguous.

5. **Role-aware design** — Three distinct user types have different needs. Approval workflows should feel different from catalog browsing, which should feel different from fulfillment management.

### Scope
All three surfaces:
- `/app/(marketing)/` — public marketing site
- `/app/(portal)/` — institution portal (requesters, approvers, finance)
- `/app/(staff)/` — internal staff/fulfillment portal

### Accessibility
WCAG AA — 4.5:1 contrast for text, keyboard navigable, screen reader friendly.
